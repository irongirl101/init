You read that right. The swiss government is open sourced; at least the Swiss Federal Chancellery. 

The govt uses an "open source by default" policy for public sector IT under the EMBAG law (Federal Law on the Use of Electronic Means for the Fulfillment of Official Tasks), which went into effect on January 1, 2024. The law mandates that federal authorities release self-developed software code under open source licenses, unless restricted by security or third-party rights.  

Key Aspects of the Swiss Open Source Policy:
- The EMBAG Law (Art. 9): Requires federal administration bodies to make publicly funded software source code freely available, championing the "Public Money, Public Code" principle.
- Exceptions: Code sharing can be blocked or limited only if vital security reasons or preexisting third-party copyrights apply.
- Open Government Data (Art. 10): Administrative non-confidential data must also be made accessible in open formats.
- Repositories & Platforms: Federal projects and policy guidelines are hosted publicly, such as on the Swiss Government GitHub Organization and the central data portal ![portal](opendata.swiss) .
They have multiple repositories, starting from lowlevel .NET libraries, geodata microservices to things I cannot read 💀; and yes, they do actively welcome public contributions and bug fixes via PRs!
